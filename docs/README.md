# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

OctoAcme uses a customer-first, iterative, and data-informed project lifecycle to turn ideas into delivery outcomes. New work begins with initiation and authorization, where the team validates the problem, identifies stakeholders, defines success metrics, and decides whether to move forward into planning. Once approved, the team creates a prioritized backlog, clarifies acceptance criteria, estimates work, captures dependencies and risks, and defines milestones and a release plan. Execution then happens through tracked work items, team rituals, quality gates, and regular reviews that keep the project aligned with the original goals.

The organization relies on clear role ownership across the lifecycle. Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, dependencies, documentation, and communication, while Product Managers define business outcomes, prioritize the roadmap, and measure success. Developers design and deliver features, QA/testing partners validate quality and acceptance criteria, and stakeholders provide input, approvals, and business context. Communication is intentionally regular and structured, using standups, weekly syncs, demos, and milestone-based status updates to surface progress, blockers, and decisions. Risk and dependency management are handled through a risk register and escalation pathways, ensuring issues move from team-level triage to project leadership when they have broader impact.

Quality and release readiness are built into the process rather than treated as an afterthought. Teams use small pull requests, automated testing, security scanning, manual QA when needed, and clear Definition of Done checks before moving work forward. Release activities include staging validation, deployment readiness checks, smoke tests, rollback planning, and stakeholder communication to reduce risk. After each sprint, release, or major milestone, the team conducts retrospectives to capture lessons learned, assign action items, and track continuous improvement across the project.
